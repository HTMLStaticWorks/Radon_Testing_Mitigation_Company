RADENZA KNOWLEDGE BASE

Radon Science & Safety Knowledge Base

Clear, evidence-based answers regarding radon testing protocols, risk levels, and mitigation system engineering.

TOP QUESTION • FUNDAMENTALS

Can I smell or see radon in my home?

No. Radon gas is completely colorless, odorless, and tasteless. It cannot be detected by normal human senses. Standard calibrated continuous monitoring devices are the only reliable method to measure indoor radon concentrations.

Key Takeaway: Continuous radon monitors (CRMs) provide precise, quantitative measurement relying on sensory detection.
SYSTEM SCIENCE • MITIGATION

How does active sub-slab mitigation work?

Active Sub-Slab Depressurization (ASD) uses a continuous inline fan connected to sealed PVC suction piping inserted beneath the concrete foundation slab. This creates negative pressure under the building, drawing soil gas out and venting it safely above the roofline.

Key Takeaway: Reverses negative pressure differentials to prevent soil gas from entering indoor living areas.
PROPERTY VARIABILITY

Does every building have identical radon levels?

No. Radon levels vary widely between properties—even between neighboring houses built on identical streets. Sub-slab soil composition, foundation micro-cracks, floor-wall expansion joints, and ventilation rates create unique readings for every structure.

Key Takeaway: Neighboring houses can have completely different radon readings due to sub-slab soil permeability.
BUILDING SCIENCE MATRIX

Radon Myths vs. Building Science Realities

Clearing up common misconceptions with factual building engineering data.

Common Misconceptions

UNSUBSTANTIATED CLAIMS
Myth #1: "My house is brand new and has a solid poured concrete basement, so radon gas cannot get inside."
Myth #2: "My next-door neighbor tested low, so my home is automatically safe from radon."
Myth #3: "Radon mitigation systems are noisy, ugly, and pull expensive heat out of the house."
Myth #4: "Radon testing is only necessary for homes with full basements, not slab-on-grade or crawlspace homes."

Building Science Realities

VERIFIED ENGINEERING FACTS
Fact #1: Concrete shrinks during curing, creating microscopic pathways. Radon enters through slab joints, sump pits, and pipe penetrations regardless of building age.
Fact #2: Sub-slab soil permeability varies significantly from lot to lot; adjacent houses frequently have completely different radon readings.
Fact #3: Modern inline fans operate silently (~40W), and suction pipes are custom color-matched or routed along exterior architectural trim.
Fact #4: Soil gas enters any building in contact with the earth, including slab-on-grade, crawlspace, and split-level structures.
PART 01

Radon Science & Soil Gas Dynamics

Understanding the geological origin, migration mechanisms, and entry pathways of sub-slab soil gas.

Q1

What is radon gas and how is it created beneath buildings?

Radon is an inert, naturally occurring gas created by the radioactive decay of trace uranium minerals naturally present in soil, granite, and rock bedrock. As uranium decays into radium and radon, the gas moves freely through subterranean soil pore spaces towards areas of lower air pressure, such as building foundations.
#SoilGas #GeologicalOrigin #DecayChain
Q2

What are the primary entry pathways into concrete foundation slabs?

Radon enters buildings through microscopic concrete slab shrinkage cracks, unsealed sump pump pits, perimeter floor-to-wall expansion joints, utility pipe penetrations, hollow concrete block walls, and unencapsulated dirt crawlspaces under negative air pressure conditions.
#SlabCracks #SumpPits #UtilityPenetrations
Q3

Why is testing necessary for every residential & commercial structure?

Because radon cannot be perceived by human sight or smell, testing is the only empirical method to determine property-specific indoor radon levels. Documented diagnostic data allows building managers and homeowners to evaluate air safety objectively.
#AirSafety #EmpiricalData #PropertyDocumentation
Q4

How do seasonal weather cycles & heating affect indoor radon levels?

Indoor radon levels fluctuate seasonally. During winter months, warm air rising inside heated buildings creates a "stack effect" vacuum that draws soil gas upward faster. Heavy rain also fills soil pore spaces, driving trapped soil gas under the foundation slab.
#StackEffect #SeasonalFluctuations #BarometricPressure
PART 02

Testing Protocols & Measurement Science

Standardized deployment protocols, closed-building requirements, and continuous monitor analytics.

Q5

What is the difference between short-term (48-hr) and long-term testing?

Short-term Continuous Radon Monitoring (48 hours to 7 days) provides rapid, high-precision hourly measurement data under closed-building conditions, ideal for real estate sales. Long-term detectors (90 days to 1 year) measure true annual average concentrations under normal living habits.
#48HrCRM #LongTerm90Days #ContinuousSampling
Q6

Where must testing equipment be placed inside a building?

Equipment is placed in the lowest lived-in area of the structure (e.g., finished basement or ground floor living area), at least 20 inches off the floor, away from exterior walls, windows, direct sunlight, kitchen exhausts, high humidity, or drafts.
#LowestLivedInLevel #PlacementStandard #Calibration
Q7

What are "Closed-Building Conditions" during a 48-hour short-term test?

Closed-building protocol requires keeping all windows and exterior doors shut for 12 hours prior to and throughout the 48-hour testing period, except for normal, brief entry and exit. Internal HVAC heating and cooling systems operate normally.
#ClosedBuildingRules #TestIntegrity #HVACSettings
Q8

How are continuous monitor results reported & what does pCi/L mean?

Radon is measured in Picocuries per Liter (pCi/L) of air. Continuous monitors log hourly concentrations and compute an overall average. The EPA reference action level is 4.0 pCi/L, with recommendations to consider mitigation for levels between 2.0 and 4.0 pCi/L.
#pCi/LUnits #EPAActionLevel #HourlyLogs
PART 03

Mitigation Engineering & System Performance

Active sub-slab depressurization mechanics, manometer pressure gauges, and quiet fan operation.

Q9

What components make up an Active Sub-Slab Depressurization (ASD) system?

An ASD system comprises a suction pit cored into the concrete slab, sealed 3" or 4" Schedule-40 PVC piping, a continuous-duty inline centrifugal fan, a U-tube manometer liquid pressure gauge, sealed foundation openings, and a vertical roof exhaust vent stack.
#ASDSystem #PVCSuctionPipe #CentrifugalFan
Q10

Does a radon mitigation fan run 24 hours a day & how much electricity does it use?

Yes. Active depressurization fans run 24/7 to maintain constant negative pressure beneath the foundation. Modern fans are energy-efficient, drawing only 40 to 80 Watts (similar to a standard household light bulb) with quiet, vibration-isolated operation.
#24-7Operation #40-80Watts #QuietMotor
Q11

How do property owners read the U-Tube Manometer pressure gauge?

The U-tube manometer mounted on the suction pipe contains colored fluid. When the fan operates, suction creates a height difference between the liquid columns. Unequal liquid levels indicate active vacuum pressure; equal liquid levels indicate fan power loss or blockage.
#U-TubeManometer #PressureGauge #SystemMonitoring
Q12

What post-mitigation verification testing is required after installation?

A 48-hour continuous verification radon test is conducted 24 hours after system activation to confirm that active sub-slab depressurization successfully reduced indoor radon concentrations below target action guidelines.
#VerificationRetest #PostMitigation #PerformanceGuarantee
PART 04

Real Estate Transactions & Property Safety

Property inspection contingencies, escrow negotiations, and long-term resale value.

Q13

How is radon testing handled during a home purchase or real estate transaction?

Radon testing is a standard contingency in property sales agreements. Certified technicians deploy tamper-monitored 48-hour continuous radon devices during the inspection window to provide documented, contract-ready reports for buyers and sellers.
#RealEstateContingency #HomeInspection #TamperMonitored
Q14

Who typically pays for radon mitigation system installation during a home sale?

If short-term test results equal or exceed 4.0 pCi/L, real estate buyers routinely request that sellers either install a professional mitigation system prior to closing or provide an escrow credit for post-closing installation.
#EscrowCredit #SellerResponsibility #ClosingTerms
Q15

What ongoing maintenance does a radon mitigation system require over time?

Active mitigation systems require minimal ongoing maintenance: checking the U-tube manometer fluid levels every few months to ensure fan suction, inspecting pipe seal joints annually, and replacing the centrifugal fan motor after its typical 8–12 year operational lifespan.
#SystemMaintenance #FanLifespan8-12Yrs #AnnualCheck
Q16

Does a radon mitigation system affect home resale value or buyer confidence?

Yes, positively. A professionally installed, certified radon mitigation system resolves buyer indoor air quality concerns, provides verified lower radon test documentation, and demonstrates proactive property building safety maintenance.
#PropertyResale #BuyerConfidence #CertifiedSafety
